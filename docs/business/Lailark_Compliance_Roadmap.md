## Assessment: enough to start domestically, not enough for the plan

For Phase 1 (home-based, testing, selling in India via own website + WhatsApp, under ₹12 lakh/year), the Basic tier is the correct legal level. But there are three gaps.

### 1. The category says you are NOT a manufacturer (fix this first)
KOB is "Food Vending Establishment" and products sit under "Other than Manufacturer Unit." A vending establishment sells/vends food; it does not manufacture it. You are making pickles, which is manufacturing. The correct KOB is **"Petty Food Manufacturer."**

As it stands, the registration does not clearly authorise you to manufacture the product you sell. Fix it via a **Modification** on FoSCoS. Highest-priority action.

### 2. Export needs a Central Licence, not this
The moment you ship internationally, Basic Registration is invalid. Exporters must hold an **FSSAI Central Licence**, regardless of turnover. Alongside it, for export you will also need:
- **IEC (Import Export Code)** from DGFT — mandatory for any export.
- For meat/seafood (beef, prawn, squid): likely **APEDA registration** and possibly plant/product approval.
- **Halal certificate** — optional but speeds Gulf customs significantly.

### 3. The ₹12 lakh ceiling will arrive fast
The revenue model reaches ~₹60,000/month (₹7.2 lakh/year) by month 3–4 and scales. Cross **₹12 lakh/year** and you must move to a **State Licence** (covers ₹12 lakh to ₹20 crore).

## Action checklist
1. **Now:** file a Modification on FoSCoS to change KOB to "Petty Food Manufacturer."
2. **Before crossing ₹12 lakh:** apply for the State Manufacturing Licence.
3. **Before first export order:** obtain Central Licence + IEC; then APEDA registration and Halal certification for beef/prawn/squid.
4. **Label compliance (all shipments):** FSSAI number, English product name, ingredients, net weight, mfg + best-before dates (printed, not hand-added), "Made in India", allergen declaration, storage instructions.
