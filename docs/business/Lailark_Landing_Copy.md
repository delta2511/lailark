## The idea the page is built on

The secret is not just love. It is measure: no ingredient is more than it should
be. That is also why the pickles pop on the tongue, one taste at a time.

Headline, in Malayalam script: **പാകത്തിന് സ്നേഹം** (love, in the right measure).

## Who the page is written for

A Malayali who knows pickle and chemmeen, enjoys the different notes of food,
feels for the story of the food, wants original traditional food kept as it is
and not engineered or optimised, and shares good food.

## Voice

- Calm storytelling, the way a narrator opens a Malayalam film. Place first,
  then the person, and each sentence leads into the next.
- Simple words. No confusing sentence structure. No short clipped lines.
- Lailark says "we" and "Sumayya".
- No em dashes.
- Mappila identity is stated plainly. Halal is said in words, as a fact about
  the house. No halal seal or logo until a certificate is held.

## Held back (true, but not in the copy)

- The dates being Sumayya's own idea. Never imply the dates are traditional, so
  no "recipes passed down" beside the prawns and dates jar.
- The Engattil name, any count of years or generations, dish names.

## 3. The pickles (DRAFT, built from Shefin's own taste descriptions)

**Prawns and dates**
It starts with a light sour from the vinegar. Then the sweetness of the dates
comes in, and after that you taste the prawn and the sea. The prawns are fried
outside and soft inside. The heat does not stay long.

**Squid**
Squid has very little taste of its own, so in this jar it carries the masala.
It begins sharp and spicy, and then it becomes calm. This is our quiet one.

**Beef**
We choose the tender parts and keep them as pieces. The outside is crisp, and
inside it is soft enough that you are not chewing forever. This one has the
most heat, and a little of it stays with you.

## 4. Two houses (AGREED)

Our family is Mappila, and our food comes from two houses. One is in Chaliyam,
where the river meets the sea, and there they knew fish. The other is in
Kunnamangalam, on the old road that brought spice down from the hills to the
Kozhikode port, and here they knew spice.

Sumayya comes from the first house and married into the second. She learned at
her mother's side, has cooked by hand for decades, and never wrote any of it
down. The fish still comes from Chaliyam, a taste from the house she grew up in.

Note: Kunnamangalam is her husband's home town, so the earlier line "grew up
cooking in both" was replaced. "Sumayya is Shefin's mother" was left out of
this section by Shefin.

## 5. പാകം (AGREED)

People say the secret of home food is love. We would say it a little
differently. പാകത്തിന്. Just enough.

Sumayya puts in just enough vinegar, just enough salt, just enough chilli, and
nothing is allowed to become more than it should be. So when you eat it, you
can taste each thing. First one, then the next.

Many pickles today are made faster and cheaper, with lighter oil and fewer
spices. We still make it the old way, with everything in it. It takes longer,
and it costs more to make.

## 6. How a jar reaches you (AGREED, Shefin's edits polished)

We do not keep jars on a shelf. Sumayya cooks a batch only when people have
asked for it.

So you book your jar first, for ₹599. When half the batch is booked, we buy
what is needed and she starts cooking, and we send you photos from the kitchen.
When it is ready, your jar is numbered by hand and sent home to you. Shipping
is free anywhere in India.

We cannot tell you the day, because the fish comes when the sea gives it. But
we buy and cook as early as we can, and send your jar as soon as it is ready.

The jars you see in stock are the ones left over from batches people booked.
Those are ₹649, and they leave the next day.

Moved out of this section, to live at checkout or in a questions block: the
quarter-of-a-batch limit, two jars per person on in-stock, and the share link.

## 7. The number on your jar (AGREED)

Every jar has a number and a QR code on its label. Scan the code and it brings
you to that batch's own page here. The page tells you where the fish came from,
the day it was cooked and bottled, and everything that went into the pot. We
never change the page later.

Batch 001: prawns from Chaliyam, landed 31 August 2026, cooked the next
morning, bottled 4 September. 22 jars.

## 8. A note from Sumayya (AGREED)

When you open the box, there is a card on top. Sumayya writes it herself, with
a pen, for you. It tells you which jar is yours and when she cooked it.

At the end she asks for one small thing. After you taste it, send her a voice
note on WhatsApp and tell her honestly how it was. She listens to every one,
and that is what makes her want to cook the next pot.

## 9. The name (FINAL)

One last thing, about the name on the jar. Neduvanchalil is our house. Nedu
means long and chal means channel. This land was once a creek, with water
coming down from the hills in the east and flowing west. The house was built
where the water used to pass, and it took the name of the land.
