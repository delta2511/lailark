# Design research for lailark.in

Reference list for the full site build. 14 Sep 2026.

Every site in section 1 to 7 was opened and checked on 14 Sep 2026. Section 8 is
from memory and the URLs need checking before you spend time on them.

This is not a list of pretty websites. It is organised by the problems you
actually have to solve: a provenance page, a drop that does not feel desperate, a
voice that sounds like a kitchen, and an orbit that has to load on 4G.

**How to use it.** For each site, do three things and nothing more. Screenshot
the one screen that works. Write one line on the single move you would steal.
Write one line on what you would not copy. If you cannot fill the second line,
the site is decoration, not research, and you can drop it. Twelve sites done this
way beats sixty bookmarked.

---

## 1. The batch page: `/batch/001` and everything after it

This is the part of your site that almost nobody else has, so the references are
thin and worth studying closely.

### Diaspora Co
https://diasporaco.com

Single-origin Indian spices, direct from named farms. The closest living relative
to what your batch page wants to be, and it is sourcing from India, so the
problems are your problems.

**Steal:** harvest date printed on every tin and repeated on the product page, so
the physical object and the website agree. Named farms treated as proper nouns,
not as a vague "our partners". The comparison table that says plainly what other
spice companies do not do.

**Do not copy:** the 150-farm scale story. You have one kitchen and two people,
and the honest version of that is stronger than a borrowed one.

### Onyx Coffee Lab
https://onyxcoffeelab.com

Lot-level detail on every coffee: producer by name, altitude, process, and they
publish tasting at 2, 5 and 10 days to show how a roast ages.

**Steal:** the discipline of publishing numbers that could embarrass you. That is
what separates transparency from a transparency campaign. Your three weights per
batch, and the yield that was 20 jars when you planned 22, belong on the site for
exactly this reason.

**Do not copy:** the density. A coffee buyer wants a spec sheet. Someone holding a
pickle jar wants four facts and a sentence.

### Tony's Chocolonely Bean Tracker
https://us.tonyschocolonely.com/pages/bean-tracker

Type the code from the wrapper, see where those beans came from.

**Steal:** the interaction itself. The wrapper is an entry point to the website,
not an advertisement for it. That is exactly your QR, and they have been doing it
long enough to have solved the usability.

**Do not copy:** the blockchain framing. It adds a technology story where a
sourcing story would do more work.

### The Whole Truth
https://thewholetruthfoods.com

Indian, and ingredient transparency is not a section of the brand, it is the
whole brand. They publish lab test reports. They have a tool for decoding
ingredient names. They say their food does not always look perfect.

**Steal:** the nerve of publishing the lab report. When you send your first
recipe out for nutrition testing, put the PDF on the site. Also the tone: plain,
slightly blunt, never precious. It is a good Indian counter-example to the soft
artisanal register most food brands default to.

**Do not copy:** the volume of educational content. That is a scale play and it
needs a content team.

## 2. Scarcity that does not feel like a tactic

You have already decided the mechanics. These are references for how the mechanics
get **explained** to a person, which is the hard part.

### Rancho Gordo Bean Club
https://www.ranchogordo.com/products/the-rancho-gordo-bean-club
https://www.ranchogordo.com/collections/out-of-stock-beans

Quarterly shipments of beans, a waiting list people genuinely brag about being
on, and sold-out beans given their own collection page called Waitlist Beans.

**Steal:** that last one, immediately. Sold-out products are not hidden, they are
a browsable list you can put your name against. It is the same instinct as your
"a sold-out hero becomes a queue, not a blank shelf", already built and proven at
a scale of one small producer.

**Do not copy:** the surprise-box format. Your buyers want prawns and dates, not a
lucky dip.

### Berry Bros & Rudd, En Primeur
https://www.bbr.com/en-primeur

Wine futures. Pay now, receive in two to three years.

**Steal:** how a page explains paying for something that does not exist yet
without apologising for it. Their framing is a plain description of the process,
not a sales pitch, and the confidence is the persuasion. Your futures price needs
the same register.

**Do not copy:** the delivery horizon or the heritage. Two to three years works
because the wine is improving. Your batch takes days, and that is better news, so
say it.

### Tree House Brewing
https://www.treehousebrew.com

Limited beer releases, announced through a dedicated releases account rather than
the main brand feed.

**Steal:** the separation. Drop announcements live on their own channel, so
following the brand does not mean being sold to daily, and following the drops
means never missing one. Worth copying into your WhatsApp setup: a batch
announcement list is a different thing from customer support.

**Do not copy:** the reliance on social media as the source of truth. Yours has to
live on your own domain, for the same reason the QR does.

## 3. Small-batch food brands with a voice

### Fishwife
https://eatfishwife.com

Note the URL. `fishwife.com` is a restaurant in a different country. Your case
studies doc should carry `eatfishwife.com`.

Tinned seafood, premium from limited runs and personality rather than from
price. Collaborations treated as news. Tins designed to be looked at.

**Steal:** the confidence that a preserved seafood product can be joyful rather
than rustic. And the collaboration pattern, which is a cheap way to make a batch
feel like an event without inventing scarcity.

**Do not copy:** the community-size boasting. One million happy anything is not
your story and does not need to be.

### Yun Hai
https://yunhai.shop

Taiwanese pantry goods. Quiet typography, generous space, producer named on the
product listing itself.

**Steal:** the restraint. This is the closest reference for how a site can carry
cultural weight without shouting, and how a producer's name in a product listing
does more than a paragraph of story elsewhere. It is the register your "small
kitchen in Kunnamangalam" line is reaching for.

**Do not copy:** nothing much. This is the single best tonal reference on the
list for Lailark.

### Graza
https://graza.co

Olive oil in squeeze bottles. Playful naming, video-forward, casual voice.

**Steal:** the product naming instinct. Drizzle, Sizzle, Frizzle tells you what
to do with the bottle in one word, and it turns a commodity into a set.

**Do not copy:** two things. The video-heavy homepage is the opposite of your 4G
constraint. And, worth noticing, they do not show harvest dates or batch numbers
on the site at all. That is a gap, and it is your opening: you can be the brand
that does say.

### Soklet
https://soklet.in

Indian tree-to-bar chocolate, single estate, Coimbatore.

**Steal:** the tree-to-bar framing as a navigable section rather than an About
paragraph.

**Do not copy:** the follow-through, or rather, notice where it stops. The
homepage promises terroir but the product listings carry no batch numbers or
estate detail. This is the most useful kind of reference: a brand with your exact
positioning that has left the specific thing you are good at on the table.

## 4. Indian brands worth an evening

Same market, same courier problems, same customer.

- **The Whole Truth**, https://thewholetruthfoods.com, covered above. The best
  Indian example of transparency as a whole brand rather than a badge.
- **Two Brothers Organic Farms**, https://gettwobrothersindia.shop (India) and
  https://twobrothersfood.com (US store). A farm selling direct, with the farm
  itself as the product story. Look at how they handle being a farm and a shop at
  the same time, which is your kitchen-and-shop problem.
- **Soklet**, https://soklet.in, covered above.

Watch for the common Indian D2C failure across all three: the homepage makes a
provenance promise and the product page does not keep it. Your batch page is the
answer to that failure, so it is worth cataloguing exactly where each one drops
the thread.

## 5. The orbit

Your section 10 wants jars rotating like planets. Before you brief anyone on
this, be clear about the cost.

Browse, do not bookmark:

- **Awwwards, Food and Drink**, https://www.awwwards.com/websites/food-drink/
- **Awwwards, Sites of the Day**, https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_day/

**How to use these honestly.** Open any orbit-style site on your phone, on mobile
data, with the wifi off. Watch how long it takes. Most of what wins awards ships
two to six megabytes of WebGL and is built for a designer's laptop on office
fibre. Your buyer is on a phone in Kerala, possibly in a shop, possibly deciding
in fifteen seconds.

Three rules to brief with, taken straight from your own doc and worth repeating
to whoever builds it:

1. The plain grid is built first and works alone. The orbit is added on top of a
   site that is already finished.
2. The orbit never blocks the first paint. If it has not loaded, the grid is
   already there and already clickable.
3. Second visit skips it. A buyer is not a guest.

If the orbit cannot be built under those three rules, the orbit is the thing that
gets cut, not the rules. A shop that loads is worth more than a shop that
impresses.

## 6. Speed and type, which is what you are actually buying

The batch page is text. No amount of art direction beats setting text well.

- Look at **Yun Hai** again, this time only at the type: measure, line height,
  the size jump between a product name and its description.
- Look at **Onyx** for hierarchy in a dense data block, which is your ingredient
  and allergen section.

The thing to take from both is that neither uses boxes or rules to separate
things. Space and size do it. Your label already works this way: the centre panel
has no boxes, only spacing and weight. The site should match the label's logic,
not just its colours.

## 7. Your own label is a reference

Do not overlook it. The printed label is the most resolved piece of Lailark
design that exists, it is in customers' hands, and it already contains a working
system:

- Three panels: story, data, identity. That is a page structure.
- A palette: black, paper white, one rust accent for the batch number, one green
  used only for claims. Four colours, each with a job.
- Two typefaces with clear roles: a monospaced face for the story, a humanist
  sans for the data.
- A rule you are already following: the data panel has no decoration at all.

The website should look like the same hand made it. If a designer proposes a
palette or a type pairing that is not derived from the label, the question to ask
is what the customer sees when they hold the jar next to the phone.

## 8. Also worth a look, not checked today

These are from memory and the URLs may have moved. Verify before spending time.

- **Burlap & Barrel**, single-origin spices, farmer stories on product pages.
- **Fly By Jing**, chili crisp, provenance plus strong voice.
- **Momofuku Goods**, pantry range, restrained commerce design.
- **Naviluna** and **Mason & Co**, Indian bean-to-bar chocolate, small-batch tone.
- **Compass Box**, Scotch whisky, ran a campaign to publish full recipe
  disclosure when the regulations discouraged it. Relevant to your percentage
  basis question and to the value of disclosing more than you must.
- **Bruichladdich**, Islay whisky, batch and cask level transparency.
- **Stripe Press**, https://press.stripe.com, books as objects on a website.
  A masterclass in making a small catalogue feel considered.
- **37signals / Once**, long-form pages that persuade with text and almost no
  JavaScript.

---

## 9. What to do with this

1. Pick six sites, not twenty. Suggested six: Diaspora Co, Yun Hai, Rancho
   Gordo, Onyx, The Whole Truth, Berry Bros en primeur.
2. For each, the three-line exercise at the top of this doc.
3. Collect the screenshots in one folder in the Lailark project.
4. Write one page called "what Lailark takes and what it refuses". The refusals
   matter more. Every brand on this list is bigger than you, and copying their
   scale moves is how a small kitchen ends up sounding like a startup.
5. Only then brief the design.
