# Sodium in Indian and Kerala Pickles: Market Benchmark

Prepared 2026-09-04 for Lailark Kitchen. Purpose: to place the Prawns & Dates
Pickle (batch 009) against what the rest of the market actually declares.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**Lailark's Prawns & Dates Pickle sits at roughly 1,005 mg sodium per 100 g. That is
about 43% below the published Indian average for pickled products, and roughly half
the level implied by the traditional 5 to 6 percent salt norm for oil pickles.**

That is a real advantage and it is worth saying on the shelf. But it comes with a
caution that matters more than the marketing: salt is one of the preservation hurdles
in a seafood pickle, and yours is carrying less of it than the category norm. Section 6
sets out what that means for the 6-month best-before decision.

One honest caveat runs through this whole report. **Indian pickle labels are poor
sources of sodium data.** The largest peer-reviewed audit found that only 75% of
pickled products declared sodium at all, and the crowd-sourced databases are worse:
Open Food Facts currently lists one Indian mango pickle at 35 mg sodium per 100 g and
another at zero, both of which are impossible. Treat every brand-level number below as
indicative, and treat the peer-reviewed category averages as the reliable spine.

---

## 2. The reliable benchmark

The best available India-wide figure comes from the George Institute's audit of packaged
foods, published in *Public Health Nutrition*.

| Category | Products | Mean sodium (mg/100 g) | Range | Declared sodium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pickled vegetables** | 57 | **1,759** | 1 to 8,000 | 75% |
| Table sauces | 62 | 1,020 | 1 to 2,400 | 53% |
| Meal-based sauces | 187 | 3,240 | 179 to 21,218 | 52% |
| Sauces and spreads, all | 572 | 2,217 | 0 to 21,218 | 47% |

Data collected 2014. It is the most recent peer-reviewed category audit found, and no
newer India-wide pickle survey appears to be published. The 2014 vintage is a genuine
limitation. The direction of travel in the same study was upward, not downward: meal-based
sauces more than doubled between 2010 and 2014.

The spread matters as much as the mean. A range of 1 to 8,000 mg/100 g inside a single
category tells you there is no such thing as a standard pickle sodium level in India.
Half that spread is real formulation difference and half is bad labelling.

---

## 3. What the salt norms imply

Industry practice for Indian pickles, from the processed food industry standards:

| Pickle type | Typical salt content |
|---|---|
| Brine pickles | 8 to 12%, sometimes 16% |
| **Oil pickles (your category)** | **5 to 6%** |
| Sweet pickles | 5 to 8% |

Regulatory floor: pickles packed in brine must carry not less than 12.0% sodium chloride.
Pickles in vinegar need acidity of not less than 2.0% as acetic acid. Oil pickles have no
minimum salt requirement, which is why the 5 to 6% figure is a norm rather than a rule.

Converting the oil-pickle norm: 5 to 6% salt is roughly **1,965 to 2,360 mg sodium per 100 g**.
That is meaningfully above the 1,759 mg measured average, which suggests the audited
products skew toward the lower-salt end of traditional practice, or that some declared
figures are understated.

## 4. Brand-level figures

These are the only Indian brand label figures that could be verified. The gaps are the story.

| Brand | Product | Sodium (mg/100 g) | Source quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mother's Recipe | Mixed Pickle | ~906 (from 2.264 g salt) | Label data, plausible |
| Mother's Recipe | Mixed Pickle (other entry) | Not declared | Incomplete |
| Tops | Mango Pickle 1 kg | "0" | Impossible, data error |
| Swastiks | Mango Pickle | "35" | Impossible, data error |
| Priya | Mango Avakaya | Not declared | Calories only |
| Double Horse | Prawn Pickle 200 g | Not published | Not on site |
| Nirapara | Prawn / Fish Pickle | Not published | Not on site |

Consumer-facing estimates, useful only as a sanity check on order of magnitude, put mango
and mixed pickle at 350 to 450 mg sodium per tablespoon. At roughly 15 g per tablespoon
that implies **2,300 to 3,000 mg per 100 g**, at the high end of the published range.

**No Kerala brand publishes sodium for its seafood pickles.** Double Horse, Nirapara and
Melam all sell prawn or fish pickle and none of them put a nutrition panel online. This is
a gap you can turn into an advantage: a printed, calculated panel is itself a differentiator
in a category where the incumbents do not publish one.

## 5. The regulatory direction

**WHO global sodium benchmark for pickled vegetables: 550 mg per 100 g.** For processed
fish and seafood the benchmark is 360 mg, and for condiments and sauces 650 mg. These are
voluntary targets, not law in India. Essentially no Indian pickle meets them. The published
Indian average is more than three times the pickled-vegetable benchmark.

**FSSAI front-of-pack warning labels are coming.** On 28 August 2026 FSSAI proposed a red
hexagonal warning mark for products high in two or more of added saturated fat, added sugar
and salt, with thresholds to be drawn from the ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024.
The numerical thresholds have not been published and the proposal is not yet enforceable.
It has to complete the regulatory process first.

**This is the thing to watch.** If the thresholds land where comparable countries have set
them, a large part of the Indian pickle category will carry a red salt mark. Being at roughly
half the category average is a good place to be standing when that happens, and it is a
reason not to raise the salt casually.

Context for the consumer: the average Indian eats about 11 g of salt a day against a WHO
recommendation of 5 g. Sodium RDA used on Indian labels is 2,000 mg per day.

## 6. Where Lailark sits

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Lailark Prawns & Dates, per 100 g | **1,005 mg sodium** (2.5 g salt equivalent) |
| Per 15 g serve | 151 mg, about 7.5% of the 2,000 mg daily RDA |
| vs Indian pickled-vegetable mean (1,759) | **43% lower** |
| vs oil-pickle salt norm (1,965 to 2,360) | **roughly half** |
| vs WHO benchmark (550) | 83% higher |
| Added salt as % of finished batch | 2.2% |

Worth knowing where the sodium actually comes from. Added salt contributes 854 mg per 100 g.
The prawns themselves contribute about 147 mg, and everything else about 4 mg. **Even with
zero added salt this pickle would sit near 151 mg per 100 g**, because prawns carry sodium
naturally. That floor is worth remembering before anyone proposes a "low sodium" variant.

### The caution

At 2.2% added salt, this pickle is below the 5 to 6% norm for oil pickles. In a vegetable
pickle that would be a straightforward health win. In a **seafood** pickle, salt is doing
preservation work alongside the oil, the vinegar and the frying-down of the prawns. You have
less of one hurdle than the category assumes.

This does not mean the product is unsafe. It means the 6-month best-before on the label is
carrying more weight than usual and should be earned by testing, not assumed. The shelf-life
protocol already in the label file (retain jars, microbial test at a NABL lab at 6 and 12
months) is the right answer and becomes more important, not less, because of the lower salt.

Note also that the sodium figure itself is still provisional: the 100 g salt weight in the
batch record has never been weighed. If the real figure is 150 g, sodium goes to 1,432 mg
per 100 g and the advantage over the category average shrinks from 43% to 19%.

---

## 7. What to do

1. **Weigh the salt on the next batch.** Everything in this report's comparison rests on
   an unweighed 100 g. It is the cheapest measurement you will ever make.
2. **Do not raise the salt without testing first.** If shelf-life testing shows you need
   more preservation hurdle, reach for the vinegar, the frying-down and the oil cover before
   the salt, so the positioning survives.
3. **Print the panel.** The Kerala incumbents do not publish nutrition for seafood pickles.
   A calculated panel on the jar is a trust signal in a category that offers none.
4. **Claim it carefully, and only after the salt is weighed.** "About half the salt of a
   typical Indian pickle" is defensible against the published average. Avoid the words "low
   sodium" and "low salt": those are regulated nutrition claims with defined thresholds under
   the FSSAI labelling regulations, and at 1,005 mg per 100 g this product does not qualify
   for either. A factual comparison is safe; a regulated claim is not.
5. **Watch the front-of-pack thresholds.** When FSSAI publishes numbers, re-run this
   comparison. It is a ten-minute job against the calculator.
